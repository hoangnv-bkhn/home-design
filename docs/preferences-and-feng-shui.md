## Research note: indoor space versus a courtyard beyond the wall

Question from owner, 2026-10-01: the intended space behind the altar wall is indoors; the family is open to an outdoor gap/courtyard if it is suitable under feng shui principles.

### Sources and limits

- [Đồ Thờ Xuân Trang — Những Bàn Thờ Gia Tiên Tốt Phong Thuỷ](https://dothoxuantrang.com/shop/nhung-ban-tho-gia-tien-tot-phong-thuy/): recommends a firm backing wall and rejects glass/window backing. This is an altar supplier's published traditional guidance, not a building standard or a school-independent authority.
- [Thiết Kế Chùa Tháp — Phong Thủy Phòng Thờ](https://thietkechuathap.com.vn/phong-thuy-phong-tho-huong-dat-ban-tho-chuan-theo-tuoi-gia-chu/): describes backing against a wall or cabinet as symbolic support. This is commercial design/practitioner guidance. Its broader allowance illustrates why the exact family interpretation matters; it does not replace this family's requirement for a wall.

Accessed 2026-10-01. Neither source establishes a specific rule permitting or forbidding a courtyard on the far side of a continuous solid backing wall. No sufficiently clear source was found in this search to declare that configuration universally favorable. Their claims about luck or outcomes are not adopted as factual predictions.

### Interpretation for this house

Distinguish the **immediate backing** from the **use on the far side of that backing**. A courtyard beyond a solid wall does not physically remove the wall. It therefore appears compatible with the narrow solid-backing principle, but that is our limited inference, not a sourced feng shui approval of this particular layout or a conclusion about every school.

Working baseline: **altar → continuous solid backing wall → quiet indoor open space**. A small indoor hall is a candidate; its use and circulation remain to be designed. Prefer a calm space over a busy route immediately behind the wall. No minimum buffer width is prescribed by the sources above; size it for the actual use.

Optional study: **altar → continuous solid exterior wall → courtyard/garden gap**. Keep this explicitly marked as an alternative with feng shui acceptance unresolved. Do not select it automatically from the owner's conditional openness. As design considerations, assess weather exposure, drainage, maintenance, privacy and noise; maintain the family's exclusions for WC/shower, gas stove and bedroom immediately behind the altar.

For the first concepts, preserve a solid wall across the altar backing zone and place any daylight openings to its sides rather than replacing that backing with glazing or an opening. This is a proposed implementation of the backing principle, not a newly discovered universal dimensional rule.

Subsequent owner clarification confirms 130° along A toward the road beside D. Use it for concept orientation; measurement method and magnetic/true north remain unspecified.
