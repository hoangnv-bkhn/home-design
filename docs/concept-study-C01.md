## What this study compares

Both options use a 10 × 10 m gross ground-floor footprint, near A–B with assumed 0.8 m offsets. Option 02 rotates the same room system 90 degrees clockwise on the plot, with window locations adjusted to favor the open yard over A's neighboring wall. This intentionally holds room areas and service stacking constant while comparing entrance, outlook and boundary relationships. It is an orientation comparison, not two independently optimized layouts.

| Topic | Option 01 — road-facing entrance | Option 02 — garden-facing entrance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main entrance | D-side forecourt | Yard toward C, approached from D |
| Living/dining outlook | Road-side yard | Deeper garden/front yard |
| Parents/brother windows | B-side gap facing neighbor's garden | D-side yard; less reliance on A-side openings |
| Grandpa/sister position | Near A/D corner | Near D/C side of house |
| Private sanitary zones | A side, ventilation strategy needed | D side, potential external ventilation |
| Stair | B/C side of house | A/B side; daylight strategy needed |
| Balcony | D-facing, through sister room | Garden-facing, through sister room |
| Geometry and room areas | Same | Same, rotated |

My preference for the next iteration is to explore **Option 02**, because the entrance and living space address the larger yard and the private wet rooms face the open road side. This is a design judgment based on the assumed site, not an owner decision. Option 01 keeps arrival more direct. Neighbor heights, opening rights, aircraft noise and actual access may change the choice.

## Rooms and close-fit issues

- F1: parents' bedroom 11.84 m² plus private sanitary/passage area; Grandpa's bedroom 11.20 m²; living/dining 15.98 m²; kitchen 6.48 m²; two separate sanitary pairs; stair; altar and indoor buffer.
- F2: brother 11.84 m², sister 11.20 m², bedroom 5 11.90 m²; two separate sanitary pairs; stair/access; utility/storage; external balcony; unfurnished zone above altar.
- Private WC and shower compartments each reserve 1.05 × 1.50 m. Shared compartments each reserve 2.40 × 1.10 m. These are compact reservations and require fixture, ventilation and door-clearance review.
- The private passage contains a short screen to break the bedroom-to-sanitary sightline. Its turning space is tight; it must be developed with actual door leaves or sliding-door details before being accepted.
- Shared WC and shower doors open separately onto their access passage, not through one another or through a bedroom. Basin locations are illustrative; shared WC contains its own basin.
- A six-seat table and modest sofa are shown. The shared living/dining space is compact for a five-bedroom family house. The furniture diagram does not prove comfortable use when all chairs are occupied.
- Stair reservation is 2.30 × 4.20 m. A possible dimensional starting point is 3.3 m floor-to-floor, 20 risers, two flights of ten risers and approximately 280 mm going. This is an assumption for fit, not a checked stair design; headroom, opening, handrails and final dimensions remain open.
- Balcony is 1.40 × 3.20 m = 4.48 m², additional to the enclosed footprint, and accessed through the sister's room. Shared balcony access would require another layout iteration.

If everyday living feels too compressed, compare a modest increase in ground-floor footprint before specifying finishes. No larger footprint or budget has been adopted by this study.

## Altar and family rules

- F1 altar zone: **3.50 m clear width × 1.70 m depth**. Width exceeds the provisional 3.4 m target; depth and ritual/furniture space need family review.
- Continuous solid backing wall; beyond it a **3.50 × 1.00 m indoor buffer**, approached from the side. No WC/shower, stove or bedroom immediately behind that wall.
- F2 reserves **3.50 × 2.80 m = 9.80 m²** as an empty area, covering both altar projection and rear buffer. No furniture or regular circulation is assigned there. This deliberately reserves more than the altar footprint; the family may choose to refine the exact exclusion boundary.
- All F2 bedroom, sanitary and balcony routes bypass the empty area. A small cleaning-access opening is shown.
- Stair flights turn away from the entrance axis; no straight entrance-to-flight alignment is intended. Confirm the visual relationship once walls/door leaves are refined.
- Optional outdoor backing courtyard is not used: the study follows the confirmed indoor-space preference.

## Site geometry and outdoor space

The editable source preserves A=15, B=16, C=13 and D=18 m. Since these do not uniquely specify the boundary, this study assumes:

1. A/B junction at (0,0); +x along A toward D; +y along B toward C.
2. A and B perpendicular.
3. C's far endpoint is 1.8 m farther in +y than B's endpoint; C stays exactly 13 m long.
4. D consists of two 9 m segments meeting at a shallow outward bend.
5. House origin (0.8,0.8), with a square 10 m footprint. Offsets are placeholders, not confirmed setbacks.
6. Orientation uses the owner's 130° along A; north display is provisional because compass convention is unknown.

This gives a model area of approximately **243.78 m²** and land outside the ground-floor footprint of approximately **143.78 m²**. Neither number is surveyed or registered area. The latter includes access strips, car accommodation and garden; it is not all usable planting space. The sketch's 192 is not used.

The site diagram includes a 2.8 × 5.5 m stationary car bay, one generic car, three scooter/bicycle symbols and a garden. The three symbols mean a preliminary allowance for “a few,” not a confirmed count. Gate location and driveway are illustrative; vehicle swept paths and maneuvering from the narrow road have not been checked. No access through a neighbor's land is assumed.

## Structure, services and daylight

- Optional dashed grid is a **discussion grid only**. It is not a column/beam plan; some axes cross rooms and need architect/engineer coordination. No member sizing, foundation selection or structural adequacy is claimed.
- Wet compartments align between floors in both options. Service markers indicate likely zones rather than sized shafts. Ducts, pipe falls, drain/vent outlets and maintenance access still need reserved space.
- Candidate windows rely on space within the plot. B's garden is not treated as permanently protected openness. The 0.8 m margins do not establish lawful opening rights or adequate light/ventilation.
- Simple massing uses a 6.6 m two-floor volume and an illustrative flat top. Roof form, parapets, facade, acoustic envelope and construction heights have not been selected.
- Browser 3D libraries are unnecessary for this comparison: the viewer includes a lightweight static SVG axonometric. The next visualization stage can add the planned interactive Three.js model after geometry is selected.
