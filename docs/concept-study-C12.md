## Arrival and wooden furniture

The entrance remains road-facing and 1.90 m wide, with outward leaves. Its lateral position changes from source y5.40–7.30 to y4.90–6.80. The front facade moves 0.40 m toward D to accommodate the coordinated layout, and the porch/canopy/steps follow. Guests arrive beside the group, then turn toward seating. The everyday stair/bedroom route continues through the cross hall behind the bench.

The low TV console sits beside the timber altar screen, perpendicular to the entrance wall. The **2.00 × 0.75 m wooden bench** faces local −y toward the altar side; the **0.75 × 0.75 m armchair** faces local −x. Both backs are away from the altar center. A **1.00 × 0.40 m tea table** is slightly offset to leave the chair's knee space usable. This is a compact wooden set, not a claim that a large traditional multi-chair suite fits.

Concept measurements:

- 1.10 m clear arrival strip beside the furniture, connecting to the 1.00 m cross hall.
- 0.55 m bench-to-table gap; 1.40 m bench-front-to-console-front distance. Actual eye-to-screen distance depends on sitting position and TV mounting.
- 0.45 m seated knee reservations at bench and armchair; ordinary arrival, altar, gallery and stair routes avoid these sampled occupied areas.
- 0.90 m gallery approach beside the bench; full 1.30 m gallery opening.
- 1.20 m front opening past the altar side screen. Actual ceremony, flame/heat separation, screen opacity and TV glare need review together.

The entrance still has a finite wall return near Grandpa's room; this proposal does not pretend it is a freestanding pavilion. Its improvement is the shifted opening, TV removal and clear side approach. A much larger receiving hall or wooden set would need a more substantial living/bedroom trade.

## Moving the shared bathrooms without losing bedroom windows

The owner's proposed move is used: both shared sanitary pairs occupy the former court bay near the kitchen/spare room. Their doors face a **1.00 m deep common side lobby**, laterally outside the stair opening. The shower door is 1.15 m laterally clear of the stair bay edge. The lobby is common space; neither bathroom opens directly into the kitchen or another bedroom. It is still a shared circulation area, not a fully enclosed toilet vestibule.

The private pairs occupy the old shared-core region, with their doors facing the private dressing space on the opposite side. Ordinary bedroom exits remain separate. A short screen blocks the tested bed-to-compartment views; routes turn around its end. Separate WC/shower counts, shower basins and floor-to-floor stacking are retained. Sliding sanitary doors remain candidates: tracks, effective openings, shower splash, basin standing space and all-position privacy need detail.

The new **4.80 m² open-to-sky court** replaces the old private-bath position beside the bedrooms. Each enlarged suite has a 1.50 m operable window across the bedroom/court wall. A common maintenance door enters the court from the quiet indoor gallery, without going through a bedroom or bathroom. Gallery fixed glazing faces this court; it is not an opening in the altar backing wall. No new A/B boundary windows are introduced.

The sky aperture is 20% larger than C11's, but this is an area comparison, **not a measured daylight or ventilation improvement**. Neighbor heights, airport noise, opening control, privacy between gallery/bedroom, drains/overflow and the when-closed air strategy remain unresolved. Gallery access to the court is occasional cleaning, not a daily through-route behind the altar.

## Opening the space behind the altar

The previous 0.90 × 3.40 m passage becomes a **1.30 × 3.40 m indoor gallery**. Removing the end ledge, opening the entire side and adding court light give it a clearer purpose as quiet breathing space and occasional maintenance access. It remains a narrow gallery, not a room for furniture or seating. The full 3.40 m depth is retained; widening is 0.40 m, about 44%.

The altar backing moves forward and stays continuous and solid. The ground worship area gives up 0.30 m depth, leaving **1.50 m in front of the 1.20 m altar reservation**. This is an explicit proposal to review against the earlier approximately 3 m depth request. Upstairs, the matching **4.08 m² empty floored strip** moves with the altar; no furniture or daily route enters it. The upper gallery keeps a closed dry linen cabinet positioned away from the court glazing. No additional family feng shui rule is introduced.

## Exterior and drawing correction

The terrace remains on the displayed right. Its 2.10 m clear depth combines 1.50 m inside the ground facade and a 0.60 m outward projection. A solid **0.60 m facade return** separates the terrace corner from the landing window. The landing opening narrows from 1.90 to 1.60 m and uses a smaller shade projection, giving that corner more room. The entry surround and thin canopy follow the relocated main door.

The reported balcony/window collision also had a renderer cause: C11 painted terrace surfaces after the foreground upper wall and window. C12 retains the existing dependency-free SVG renderer and orders overlapping surfaces by depth along the axonometric camera ray. Slab, guards, roofs, window glass and foreground walls now occlude each other consistently. Both shade variants resolve without ordering cycles in the browser check. This is a depiction fix, not a structural collision/engineering analysis.

Both options use **the same plan**: 01 partial pergola; 02 thin full shelter roof. Option 02 remains the assistant's preferred direction for shade/rain-cover development; neither is owner-selected. The restrained pale walls, charcoal stair surround, warm entry and open metal guards are retained. Actual roof support, guarding, waterproofing, threshold details and neighbor privacy still require design.

## Area, usability and coordination costs

The bounding house changes from 10.50 × 11.40 to **10.90 × 11.40 m**; B stays longer than A. Ground area is 124.26 m² outer envelope less 4.80 m² court = **119.46 m²**. F2 subtracts the 7.84 m² terrace recess = **111.62 m²**, including stair reservation. The 9.66 m² terrace and its optional roof remain construction. These are concept measurement conventions, not contractual/statutory floor areas.

The 0.40 m front extension reduces modeled front depth along A from 4.40 to **4.00 m**. Rear/A allowances, parcel shape and car/gates stay provisional. The porch remains 2.20 × 2.20 m, canopy 2.45 × 2.80 m; three 150 mm rises and two 300 mm treads still rely on the assumed yard level. Two-wheel reservations move outward with the facade. Stationary fit and walking checks do not establish vehicle turning.

The side lobby displaces 1.21 m² of the former kitchen counter strip. Named kitchen/dining area is **12.21 m²**, with a 0.37 m² open join; compact six-chair footprints remain. Counter run is 2.70 m, fridge separate. Simultaneous cooking, chair withdrawal and appliance opening are the main remaining usability weakness. Grandpa/sister gain the facade extension and become **16.28 m² each**; spare stays **12.58 m²**.

This arrangement prioritizes the new bedroom minimum and the requested arrival/bathroom improvements over C11's tighter wall alignment. Suite wall x4.05 is offset 0.40 m from kitchen/stair x3.65; the private wet/gallery edge is x6.55 while stair/Grandpa remains x6.25. These are architectural references, not engineered axes. Court and terrace support/load paths require professional coordination. Ground area is 19.46 m² over the approximate 100 m² target; affordability remains unassessed.

Alternatives considered: merely moving the front door would leave the TV/seating conflict; simply covering the old court would remove the suites' only normal windows; enlarging bedrooms without moving the private core would squeeze the altar buffer further. The coordinated swap addresses those linked problems, at the area and alignment costs above.
