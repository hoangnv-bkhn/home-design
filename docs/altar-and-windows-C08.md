# C08 — altar depth and normal windows

Access/review date: 2026-10-03. Cultural guidance and general building physics; no new Vietnamese statutory dimension or compliance claim.

## Altar depth

The owner finds the 1.50 m depth inadequate and suggests approximately 3 m; also asks whether exchanging the altar and Grandpa room would help. C08 proposes **3.4 m width × 3.0 m depth**. A modeled 0.60 m altar cabinet leaves **2.40 m forward depth**, with 0.60 m at each side of its 2.20 m width. Actual altar furniture, kneeling/standing arrangements and ceremony group size remain unknown. These dimensions are practical reservations, not an auspicious numerical calculation.

Reviewed sources:

- [Phong thuỷ và chuyện thiết kế — Tạp chí Kiến Trúc, 6 July 2012](https://www.tapchikientruc.com.vn/chuyen-muc/phong-thuy-va-chuyen-thiet-ke.html), sections on rooms and reconciling design with cultural practice. Architectural commentary hosted by the Vietnam Association of Architects' journal; qualitative, not a building code or a universal feng shui method. It discusses the family's practical requirements alongside cultural choices. It does not establish a 3 m altar-room depth.
- [Bài trí phòng thờ chung với phòng khách — Tạp chí Kiến trúc Việt Nam](https://kientrucvietnam.org.vn/bai-tri-phong-tho-chung-voi-phong-khach-nhu-the-nao-de-tranh-dai-ky/), author Tuyết Ngân, compiled article, publication date not established. Sections on quiet placement and worship furniture/screens support treating worship as a calm, maintained part of the home. This is secondary cultural/design commentary, not a measured rule or school-independent authority. Claims about fortune are not adopted. It establishes no universal 3 m depth.

Limited finding: **no universal 3 m depth requirement was established by these sources**. That is not proof that no adviser or tradition uses one. Keep the family's existing rules: SE/road facing, solid wall, indoor space beyond it, empty F2 floor over the complete designated altar zone, and sofa backs away from altar. No new direction, birth-based prescription or Lỗ Ban constraint is added.

A direct exchange places the deeper altar in the front/C bedroom bay. Its required indoor backing buffer then competes with the stair bay, and the entire 10.20 m² upper reservation would displace the sister room. The parents room still lacks a real window. This is a topology assessment, not a complete second design or a feng shui prohibition. C08 enlarges both altar and Grandpa in their existing parts of the house while relocating the wet zones and adding a separate court. Neither arrangement has owner approval.

## Normal opening windows

- [Windows — US Department of Energy, Windows Technologies Subprogram](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/windows), “Windows Research” section, no publication date shown. Primary institutional general guidance describes daylight, views and natural ventilation as window functions. It supports the owner's reason for preferring windows; it supplies no Hanoi opening permission or quantitative air exchange for this house.
- [Natural Ventilation and Cooling — DOE Building Science Education](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/natural-ventilation-and-cooling), “What / Why / How” sections, no publication date shown. Primary general guidance explains wind and buoyancy-driven airflow and the dependence on appropriate opening arrangements and outdoor conditions. This is building-physics education, not Vietnamese residential code or a climate-specific performance calculation.

Design inference: an opening window to outside air can provide a view and a ventilation opportunity that a sealed reflective daylight tube cannot. **An opening window does not prove adequate ventilation.** The parents room has one courtyard-facing wall; cross ventilation with its ordinary bedroom door shut is not demonstrated. No air changes/hour, lux, daylight factor, cooling savings or code compliance is asserted.

C08 uses a **3.4 × 2.0 m / 6.80 m² own open-to-sky court**, with ground paving and **no F2 slab or roof across it**. It is beside the sleeping rooms and separate from the indoor altar buffer. Parents and brother receive a nominal **1.80 × 1.65 m window**, with sliding/tilt panel candidates. Upper sill is 0.90 m, lower 0.75 m; these are proposals, not verified guard heights. The court provides a limited courtyard view, rather than a distant garden view. Curtains/screens and inter-floor sound/privacy need assessment.

Two **1.60 × 1.60 m stair windows** face the own C yard, one on each storey, with operable panel candidates. Sill position relative to actual flights/landing, guard/limiter, cleaning reach or remote hardware and rain safety need architect detailing. A nominal 1.0 m floor-relative sill does not certify stair protection.

Bedroom 5 moves to the C-facing bay and receives a normal window. Reflective tubes, upper chase boxes and the old rooflight are removed. No A/B boundary windows or new opening permission are inferred from the reported gaps or neighbor's garden.

## What remains open

The court is a small two-storey lightwell. Sky access, neighbor wall heights, wind, aircraft noise, heat/humidity, insects, glazing/shading and usable opening area need assessment. Window airflow is conditional on outdoor conditions and opening operation. Provide a separate when-closed ventilation strategy in services design; no equipment is selected here.

Ground cleaning access is through the parents room via a separate 0.75 m court door; that is an explicit privacy/maintenance compromise. Court drains, waterproof thresholds, overflow route, roof/parapet edges and access must be designed. Bath/kitchen extract placement should avoid recirculation into sleeping windows; no extract terminal is adopted. Wet stacks and window/court structural edges remain preliminary. Setbacks, actual opening rights, local planning/airport controls and professional review remain S02/E01/E02, unchanged in evidence status.
